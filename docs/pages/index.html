Everything that matters about your child, in a place anyone can understand

Larova is an app you set up on your child's phone. You fill it with the things you would otherwise have to explain — how bedtime works, what your child eats, who to call, what helps when they are upset.

Hand someone the phone, without a word of explanation

Caregiver view is what the app normally shows: read, tick things off, make a call, open a link. Nothing can be changed, moved or deleted by accident.

Parent view unlocks with a PIN or your fingerprint, and only there do the edit handles and the backup options appear. After five minutes without input, the app returns to caregiver view by itself.

A bar on every screen that says Get help

Behind it are the numbers you chose, shown large, with a photo and a relation — “Mum”, “Dad”, the doctor, the local emergency number.

Tapping one opens the phone app with the number ready. It never dials on its own, and it never sends anyone's location anywhere. The person holding the phone decides.

What it actually looks like

These are not mock-ups. They are rendered from the app's own code on every re-record, alongside the screenshot tests, so this page cannot show a version of Larova that no longer exists.

  • The start screen. Everything on it was written by a parent.
  • A guide, one step at a time, at reading size.
  • A checklist. Ticking off needs no unlocking.
  • Behind “Get help”: the numbers you chose, and nothing else.
  • Night mode, for reading a bedtime guide aloud in the dark.

Ten kinds of tile, arranged by you

Everything inside a tile is written by you. Larova provides the shape; you provide the content.

  • GuideNumbered steps, one per screen, each with text and optionally a picture or a recording.
  • Video and audioFrom the gallery, or recorded on the spot in your own voice.
  • NoteFree text with headings and bullet points.
  • ChecklistItems to tick off, optionally resetting each morning.
  • TableColumns and rows you define yourself.
  • CallA name, a number and a photo. One tap to dial.
  • WebsiteA link that opens in the browser.
  • App shortcutOpens another app already installed on the phone.
  • FolderGroups tiles together.

Nothing leaves the device. There is nowhere for it to go.

Larova stores everything on the phone and nowhere else. No account, no analytics, and no internet permission at all — not a setting, an absent Android permission. Nothing you write is ever sent to us, because there is no “us” to send it to.

One menu item packs every tile, picture and recording into a single file. You choose where it goes: the phone itself, a cloud drive, a USB stick. That same file is the handover package — send it to your partner or the other grandparents, they open it in their own copy, and everything is there. You can protect it with a password.

A night mode that exists for one specific reason

Reading a bedtime guide aloud in a darkened room from a white screen does not work. Night uses warm amber on near-black, dims the display and avoids bright surfaces entirely, so nobody wakes up.

Fourteen languages, set independently of the phone

A grandmother who does not read German can put Larova into Turkish while her grandchild's phone stays in German. The app is translated; what you write is not — your words stay exactly as you wrote them, and are never sent anywhere to be translated.

Larova is a notebook. It does not diagnose anything, does not calculate doses, does not interpret what you write, and does not give advice. If you use it to note down what a caregiver should know about your child's health, that note is your text — the same as the one you would tape to the fridge. Larova just carries it and shows it to the right person at the right moment.

It is not a medical device, and it is not an emergency service. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.
