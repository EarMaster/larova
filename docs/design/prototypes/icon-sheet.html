A niche where the light stays on.

Larova comes from the Lares, the household spirits of Roman homes. Their shrine was the lararium: a small arched niche in the wall holding a lamp that burned as long as the house was lived in. That shape is the icon.

The shape does three things at once, none of which needs explaining.

It is a shrine. The arch is the niche of the Lares, so the meaning of the name sits inside the image rather than beside it.

It is a doorway with the light left on. Someone is there even when you are not. That is the entire product in one mark.

It is a nightlight. That ties the icon to the leading use case, bedtime, and to the night mode built for it.

Three variants

All three use the same arch. They differ in what stands inside the niche.

Recommended

A · The niche

Arch, threshold, lamp. The most reduced version and the only one that stays fully legible at 24 pixels. The glow on the wall gives the mark depth without adding a shape.

B · Niche with tiles

Three tiles stand in the niche instead of the lamp, in the same arrangement as the start screen. It says more about the function but loses the light, and below 48 pixels the tiles merge into a blur.

C · The guardian

The niche becomes a figure: a small guardian with a light inside. The warmest and most charming of the three, but the filled shape feels heavier and can read as a headstone against some backgrounds.

Safe zone

Android scales and clips adaptive icons. The canvas is 108dp; only the inner 66dp circle is guaranteed to stay visible.

  • The outermost point of the drawing sits 32 dp from the centre against a safe radius of 33 dp. It fits, with little margin at the ends of the threshold.
  • The layers ship as icon-background.svg, icon-foreground.svg and icon-monochrome.svg. Import them in Android Studio via New → Image Asset, which produces the VectorDrawable files and ic_launcher.xml.
  • The monochrome layer belongs in the same ic_launcher.xml as <monochrome>. Without it, themed icons fall back to a grey placeholder.
  • The Play Store listing additionally needs a 512 × 512 PNG with no transparency and no corners of its own — the square at the top left is that version.

Deliberately left out

No child, no hand, no heart. Those three motifs occupy nearly every family app and make an icon indistinguishable in a grid. The arch is a rare shape in the Play Store and therefore easy to find again on a crowded home screen.

No letter L. An initial explains only the name and says nothing about the product — and with an invented word nobody knows yet, there is nothing to recognise.
